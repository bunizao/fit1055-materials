## Slide 29: Step 2: Design Solution – Sample Answer “Today I Am Paul”

- Step 2: Design Solution – Sample Answer “Today I Am Paul”
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 7
- STEP 1 OUTPUT Violations: Deception, Autonomy, Privacy | Stakeholders: Mildred, Family, Care Staff
- 2.1 EMPATHIZE
- Primary Users
- Mildred (patient) – cannot give full informed consent; vulnerable, cognitively declining
- Paul (AI caregiver) – operates without explicit identity disclosure
- Secondary Stakeholders
- Family members – deceived about who is caring for their loved one
- Care facility staff – responsible for ethical deployment; accountability gap
- Broader society – norms around AI identity and elder care trust
- 2.2 IDEATE – Solution Types
- 💻 Technical Solutions
- AI identity disclosure module: Paul announces its AI status at session start
- Consent-tracking algorithm: logs and validates ongoing patient permissions
- Privacy-preserving data architecture: health data encrypted, access audited
- 📋 Policy Solutions
- Mandatory AI disclosure policy: facilities must inform patients/families of AI caregivers
- Family consent protocol: written opt-in required before AI caregiver deployment
- ACM 1.3 compliance guideline: honesty and transparency as non-negotiable operating rules
- 📱 UX Design Solutions
- Visual AI indicator: subtle persistent on-screen icon showing “AI Companion Active”
- Family dashboard: real-time transparency into Paul’s interactions and care logs
- Gradual transparency interface: adapts disclosure to Mildred’s cognitive capacity
- STEP 2 OUTPUT: Redesigned AI caregiver system (Paul) with transparent identity disclosure, family consent mechanisms, privacy-preserving care protocols, and adaptive UX — addressing ACM 1.1 (wellbeing), 1.3 (honesty) and 1.6 (privacy) violations. Feeds into Step 3: Implement.
- ACM Violations Addressed
- ACM 1.1 – Contribute to wellbeing: Paul deceives Mildred, causing psychological harm
- ACM 1.3 – Be honest and trustworthy: impersonates human carers and family members
- ACM 1.6 – Respect privacy: collects and processes intimate personal health data without consent
- ACM 2.5 – Give comprehensive evaluation: facility staff not informed of AI deployment risks

## Slide 30: Step 2 Output: Proposed Product Solution “Today I Am Paul”

- Step 2 Output: Proposed Product Solution “Today I Am Paul”
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 7
- Selected from ideation: The Technical solution is chosen as the proposed product because it directly eliminates the root cause (identity deception) through the system itself, rather than relying on external policy or user interface alone.
- Ethical AI Companion System (Paul v2.0)
- A transparent, consent-driven AI caregiving platform for elder care
- Feature 1 — Identity Disclosure Module
- Paul verbally and visually identifies itself as an AI at every session start and whenever asked. A persistent on-screen indicator remains active throughout.
- Addresses: ACM 1.3 (honesty) — eliminates identity deception directly at the system level
- Feature 2 — Adaptive Consent Engine
- Tracks Mildred’s cognitive capacity in real time to calibrate the transparency level of disclosures. Family consent dashboard gives relatives oversight and control.
- Addresses: ACM 1.1 (wellbeing) + ACM 1.6 (privacy) — protects autonomy of a vulnerable user
- Feature 3 — Privacy-Preserving Care Log
- All health and interaction data is encrypted, access-audited, and only shared with authorised care staff. Mildred’s data is never used to train AI models without explicit opt-in.
- Addresses: ACM 1.6 (privacy) + ACM 2.5 (evaluation) — closes the accountability gap for care staff
- Why this solution has most impact
- Fixes the root cause in the system, not just the surface behaviour — deception cannot occur if the code prevents it
- Protects the most vulnerable stakeholder (Mildred) directly — adapts to cognitive capacity
- Addresses the most ACM violations (1.1, 1.3, 1.6, 2.5) in a single coherent product
- Policy and UX solutions are incorporated as supporting features, not alternatives
- Users incorporated in the solution
- Mildred — receives adaptive, honest AI interaction scaled to her capacity
- Family — given oversight via consent dashboard and interaction logs
- Care staff — notified of AI deployment scope and accountable through audit trail
- Society — establishes a replicable ethical standard for AI elder care
- Proposed Product Solution: Ethical AI Companion System (Paul v2.0) — a redesigned AI caregiver with built-in identity disclosure, adaptive consent management, and a privacy-preserving care log. This solution is prioritised because it resolves the primary ethical violation (deception under ACM 1.3) at the system level, directly protects the most vulnerable stakeholder (Mildred), and brings all identified parties — patient, family, and care staff — into an accountable, transparent care relationship.

## Slide 34: Summary of learnings today

- Summary of learnings today
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 7
- 1. Complex Problem Solving
- Ethical issues in computing involve multiple stakeholders, competing values, and unintended consequences
- Simple problems don't exist at a professional scale
- 2. Ethical reasoning framework is systematic process to guide you in making ethical-technical decisions
- Like debugging or algorithm design, ethical reasoning requires systematic frameworks
- Intuition alone isn't sufficient for professional-level decisions
- 3. Values Integration is Unavoidable
- Every technical choice embeds values about privacy, autonomy, truth, and fairness. The question isn't WHETHER to include values, but whether you choose them consciously
