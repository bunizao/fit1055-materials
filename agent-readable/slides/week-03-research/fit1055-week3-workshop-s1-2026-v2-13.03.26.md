- Introduction to Intelligent research cycle
- Research is not a one-time event — it is iterative. Each stage builds toward professional-quality intelligence.
- Stage 1
- Identify a topic, understand the landscape through literature review, and develop a research question
- Stage 2
- Choose methodologies — data collection, critical analysis, interviews, or other approaches based on your topic
- Stage 3
- Draw conclusions and share knowledge through reports, publications, or recommendations to stakeholders
- The Intelligent Research Cycle
- A model for converting raw data and information into actionable intelligence for an organisation.
- 6 Stages: Direction → Collection → Processing → Analysis → Dissemination → Feedback

## Slide 11: FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3

- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3
- Stage 2: Types of Research Sources
- Before you search, understand what type of source you are looking for:
- PRIMARY
- First-hand, original sources — not filtered or interpreted by others.
- When to use:
- To make original claims or gain direct evidence.
- Examples:
- Interviews, original code, conference papers, patents
- SECONDARY
- Build on primary sources with in-depth analysis and interpretation.
- When to use:
- Background context, see what others have discussed, explore subtopics.
- Examples:
- Textbooks, review articles, blog posts
- TERTIARY
- Distillations of primary and secondary sources — compiled for broad overview.
- When to use:
- General overview, background information, highly filtered for accuracy.
- Examples:
- Encyclopedias, dictionaries, handbooks
- For our scenario: Begin with 1-2 secondary sources (case studies on ethical issues) then find 1-2 primary sources on ChatGPT techniques.

- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3
- Stage 2: Search Strategies
- Knowing how to search is as important as knowing what to search for.
- Google Search Operators
- Use operators to refine your search and filter results:
- "exact phrase" — search for this exact phrase
- site:edu — search only educational sites
- filetype:pdf — find PDFs only
- after:2022 — results published after 2022
- Example: "ChatGPT ethics" site:edu after:2023
- Monash Library Search
- Use Boolean operators for database searches:
- AND — narrow search (both terms must appear)
- OR — broaden search (either term)
- NOT — exclude a term
- Use " " for exact phrases
- For our scenario: "ChatGPT" AND "healthcare" AND "ethics"
- Tip: Every search engine uses variants of Boolean operators. Learning search strategies saves significant time during Assignment 1.

- Stage 4: Evaluating Sources — CRAAS Framework
- Not all sources are equal. Use CRAAS to assess quality before you rely on a source.
- C
- Currency
- Is the info still relevant and recent for the topic?
- R
- Relevance
- Does it directly address your research question?
- A
- Accuracy
- Can you verify this with other sources? Is it correct?
- A
- Authority
- Who wrote it? Do they have genuine expertise in this area?
- S
- Stability
- Is this info stable over time, or is it volatile and frequently changed?

- Stage 4: Integrating Sources & Finding Patterns
- Analysis = evaluating + integrating + identifying patterns across multiple sources.
- How to Integrate Sources
- Start with secondary sources — use their reference lists to find primary sources
- Highlight key findings that relate to your research objective
- Identify patterns: What are the common themes across sources?

## Slide 18: FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3

- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3
- APA-7 Citation Quick Guide
- Format: Author(s), Last, F. M. (Year). Title of work. Source. DOI/URL
- 📄 Journal Article
- Author(s). (Year). Title. Journal, Vol(Issue), pp-pp. DOI
- Haltaufderheide, J., & Ranisch, R. (2024). The ethics of ChatGPT in medicine and healthcare. Digital Health, 10(1). https://doi.org/...
- 🎤 Conference Paper
- Author(s). (Year, Month). Title. In Proceedings (pp. xx-xx). Publisher.
- Vaswani, A., et al. (2017, December). Attention is all you need. In Proceedings of NeurIPS 2017 (pp. 5998-6008). Curran Associates.
- 📘 Book
- Author(s). (Year). Title of book (Ed. if applicable). Publisher.
- Floridi, L. (2019). The logic of information: A theory of philosophy as conceptual design. Oxford University Press.
- 🌐 Website
- Author(s) or Org. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Site Name. URL
- Australian Computer Society. (2023, March 15). ACS code of ethics. ACS. https://www.acs.org.au/code-of-ethics
- 📰 News Article (Online)
- Author(s). (Year, Month Day). Title of article. Outlet. URL
- Metz, C. (2024, February 8). OpenAI tests AI agent that can take over your computer. The New York Times. https://www.nytimes.com/...
- 📋 Report / Government Doc
- Author(s) or Dept. (Year). Title (Report No.). Publisher. URL
- Australian Government. (2023). Safe and responsible AI in Australia: Discussion paper. Department of Industry. https://...
- ✦ Hanging indent: first line flush, rest indented 0.5"
- ✦ Alphabetical order by first author's surname
- ✦ DOI preferred over URL; omit if unavailable
- ✦ No 'Retrieved from' unless content changes over time

## Slide 21: FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3

- Report Structure Overview
- A strong report has a logical, narrative structure that flows from context → evidence → conclusion.
- 1
- Introduction
- Context, definitions, preview of paper
- 2
- Literature Review
- What others have found — integrated analysis of sources
- 3
- Problem Statement
- Define the problem, why it matters, attempts to fix it
- 4
- Conclusion
- Restate problem, summarise findings, key takeaways
- 5
- References
- APA-7 format, all cited sources
- The title of your investigation should reflect your MAJOR FINDING — not just the topic. E.g. ChatGPT's potential impact in healthcare: Ethical considerations in choosing Large Generative Models

## Slide 22: FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3

- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3
- Section: Introduction
- The introduction contextualises your work so that even a reader without background can understand why it matters.
- What to include:
- Define all key concepts (e.g. Ethical AI, Artificial Intelligence, LLMs)
- State the significance: Why does this topic matter? Who is affected?
- Provide 1-2 compelling examples or case studies to motivate the investigation
- End with a preview: 'In this report, we will first review… then… and conclude with…'
- Good intro structure (from our scenario):
- 1. Define Ethical AI and LLMs → 2. State healthcare applications → 3. Show evidence of real harm (case studies with citations) → 4. Preview the paper's structure

## Slide 23: FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3

- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3
- Section: Literature Review
- A literature review synthesises what others have found — it is analysis, not just summary.
- Purpose
- Show familiarity with existing scholarship on your topic
- Identify gaps, contradictions, and consensus in the research
- Build the theoretical foundation for your Problem Statement
- Develop your own analytical narrative (synthesis)
- How to write it
- Select studies with compelling evidence for your argument
- Describe findings + limitations in your own words
- Connect studies to each other — show how they relate
- If explaining how a technology works (e.g. LLMs), paraphrase at a high level accessible to any reader
- Always cite — every claim needs a source

## Slide 24: FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3

- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3
- Section: Problem Statement
- The problem statement addresses what the problem is, who is affected, why it matters, and why existing solutions are insufficient.
- Part 1 - HOW
- How things SHOULD work under ideal situations?
- "ChatGPT, ideally, would improve ER waiting times by helping practitioners triage patient notes in real-time, saving up to 20% of waiting time."
- Part 2 - WHAT
- What are the specific ethical issues that arose from the real-world AI deployment?
- "However, ChatGPT has shown bias in triage, affecting patient outcomes and posing danger — particularly for minority populations."
- Part 3 - WHY
- Why did these ethical issues arise from the AI implementation? What is the root cause?
- "Although efforts address hallucinations and bias, issues remain in how data is processed — careful ethical consideration is required before deployment."

## Slide 25: FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3

- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 3
- Section: Conclusion
- The conclusion wraps up your investigation and leaves the reader with a strong final impression.
- What to include:
- 1. Restate the problem statement addressed in the paper
- 2. Summarise your main findings from the literature review
- 3. State the key takeaways: why is this topic significant? What technical AI issue must developers consider?
- From our scenario:
- "We highlighted studies on ChatGPT in healthcare — showing how it can improve emergency department efficiency when used ideally, but given the current limitations (hallucination, bias), ethical consequences are significant. Future developers should understand the problem domain and technical specifics before deploying AI in critical settings."
