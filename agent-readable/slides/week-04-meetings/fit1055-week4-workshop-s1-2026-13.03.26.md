## Slide 4: What is a Meeting?

- What is a Meeting?
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 4
- A purposeful gathering to discuss, coordinate, and accomplish shared objectives.
- Definition
- A meeting is a structured conversation where people come together to share information, make decisions, and coordinate action.
- Meetings are the primary communication mechanism in every IT team — from daily standups to board-level strategic reviews.
- What makes a meeting effective?
- ▪ Clear purpose and agenda ▪ Right people in the room ▪ Documented outcomes (minutes) ▪ Action items with owners & deadlines
- Why Meetings Matter for IT Professionals
- 🗂 Align teams — on sprint goals, project scope, and technical decisions
- 🤝 Build relationships — with clients, stakeholders, and cross-functional teams
- 🔍 Solve problems — collaboratively and make decisions with full context
- 📣 Communicate progress — and blockers to management and peers
- 🚀 Drive careers — by demonstrating leadership, communication, and initiative

## Slide 5: Formal vs. Informal Meetings

- Formal vs. Informal Meetings
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 4
- Both types are common in IT workplaces — knowing the difference shapes how you prepare.
- FORMAL
- Pre-planned with structured agenda, chairperson, formal notice, and written minutes.
- When to use:
- Strategic decisions, stakeholder reviews, board meetings, project sign-offs
- Examples:
- Board meetings, AGMs, sprint reviews, client requirement sessions, EGMs
- INFORMAL
- Less structured — no required agenda, chairperson, or minutes. Direction can shift.
- When to use:
- Quick problem-solving, information sharing, team alignment, brainstorming
- Examples:
- Daily standups, pair-programming sessions, hallway chats, team check-ins
- VIRTUAL
- Any formal or informal meeting conducted via Zoom, MS Teams, Google Meet.
- When to use:
- Distributed teams, remote work, cross-timezone collaboration
- Examples:
- Remote standups, client demos, online interviews, hybrid team meetings

## Slide 11: Why Discussion Methods Matter in Meeting

- Why Discussion Methods Matter in Meeting
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 4
- Not every meeting needs the same voice.
- Effective IT professionals choose discussion methods intentionally — matching the technique to what the meeting actually needs to achieve.
- The wrong method wastes time. The right one accelerates decisions, surfaces hidden concerns, and distributes voice fairly across the team.
- Before choosing a discussion method, ask:
- 01
- What outcome do we need?
- Are you generating ideas, making a decision, surfacing concerns, or assigning ownership? Each requires a different structure.
- 02
- Who needs to contribute?
- Some methods favour vocal participants. Others — like go-around or spend-a-dollar — ensure quieter team members have equal weight.
- 03
- How much time do we have?
- Brainstorming is open-ended. List-and-commit is fast and closure-focused. Matching method to time budget keeps meetings productive.

## Slide 13: Meeting Minutes — Purpose & Process

- Meeting Minutes — Purpose & Process
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 4
- Minutes transform conversation into accountability. Without them, you have no recourse.
- What minutes must capture
- 📌 What was decided
- Decisions, resolutions, outcomes agreed by the group
- ✅ What needs to be done
- Action items — specific tasks with clear owners
- 📅 By when
- Deadlines for every action item — no ambiguity
- ⚠ Without minutes, you may need to repeat the entire meeting if actions aren't completed.
- Minutes creation process
- 1
- Pre-planning Use the agenda as your minutes template — start before the meeting
- 2
- Record-taking Clarify expected level of detail with chair before starting
- 3
- Writing Capture decisions, accomplishments, and all future actions
- 4
- Sharing Distribute within 24 hours via email, Teams, or Google Drive
- 5
- Storage File in accessible location for future reference and accountability

## Slide 19: The STAR Technique — Structured Communication

- The STAR Technique — Structured Communication
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 4
- A framework for presenting experiences clearly in meetings, interviews, performance reviews, and cover letters.
- S
- Situation
- Set the scene — describe the context and background so your audience understands the circumstances.
- T
- Task
- State your specific responsibility — what were you asked or required to do?
- A
- Action
- Explain the steps you personally took — focus on your contribution, not the team's.
- R
- Result
- Describe the outcome — ideally quantified. What changed because of your actions?
- T

## Slide 20: STAR in Action — Software Bug Scenario

- STAR in Action — Software Bug Scenario
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 4
- Behavioural question: "Tell me about a time you solved a critical problem under pressure."
- S
- Situation: During my final-year project, we discovered a critical authentication bug 48 hours before our beta launch to 200 test users. The bug exposed user session tokens and could have caused a full security breach.
- T
- Task: As the security lead on the team, I was responsible for identifying the root cause, coordinating the fix, and ensuring we met the launch deadline without compromising user safety.
- A
- Action: I called an emergency standup, assigned debugging tasks based on team expertise, implemented a temporary session invalidation patch within 4 hours, and documented the vulnerability for our final report.
- R
- Result: We launched on time with zero security incidents. The client gave us the highest feedback rating in the cohort. The patch approach was later adopted as a standard process by our supervisor.

## Slide 21: STAR — When to Use & Best Practices

- STAR — When to Use & Best Practices
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 4
- STAR works beyond interviews — it is a general professional communication framework.
- When to use STAR
- 💼 Job interviews — Behavioural questions: 'Tell me about a time…'
- 📋 Performance reviews — Demonstrate impact with evidence
- 📝 Cover letters — Structure your 'experience' paragraph
- 🗣 Meeting updates — Report on progress with clear outcomes
- 🤝 Client presentations — Show your problem-solving credibility
- 🎓 Assessment tasks — FIT1055 oral presentations and reflections
- Tips for effective STAR responses
- ✦ Keep it concise — aim for 90–120 seconds verbally
- ✦ Use a conversational tone — avoid sounding rehearsed
- ✦ Quantify results where possible (%, time saved, users impacted)
- ✦ Focus on YOUR specific contribution — not the team's
- ✦ Choose examples that highlight skills relevant to the role
- ✦ Prepare 3–4 strong examples that can flex across question types
- ✦ Anticipate follow-up questions: 'What would you do differently?'
