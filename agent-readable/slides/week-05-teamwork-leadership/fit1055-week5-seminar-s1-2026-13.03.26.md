## Slide 11: Tuckman's Five Stages of Team Development

- Tuckman's Five Stages of Team Development
- Bruce Tuckman (1965) — all teams progress through these stages before reaching peak performance.
- 1
- Forming
- Behaviour:
- Cautious, polite, exploring
- Focus:
- Getting acquainted
- Leadership:
- Directive — provide clear structure and guidance
- 2
- Storming
- Behaviour:
- Conflict, disagreement, power struggles
- Focus:
- Establishing roles
- Leadership:
- Supportive coaching — facilitate conflict resolution
- 3
- Norming
- Behaviour:
- Cooperating, establishing standards
- Focus:
- Process definition
- Leadership:
- Collaborative facilitation — help establish norms
- 4
- Performing
- Behaviour:
- Collaborative, anticipating needs
- Focus:
- Goal achievement
- Leadership:
- Delegative oversight — empower and step back
- 5
- Adjourning
- Behaviour:
- Reflecting, separating
- Focus:
- Closure & evaluation
- Leadership:
- Reflective leadership — celebrate and evaluate

## Slide 12: Applying Tuckman's Model in Practice

- Applying Tuckman's Model in Practice
- Use the model as a diagnostic tool — not just a framework to memorise.
- ▪ Diagnose your stage: Honestly assess where your team is right now based on actual behaviour — not intentions
- ▪ Set realistic expectations: Development takes time; don't expect Performing-level output in Forming
- ▪ Plan targeted interventions: Stuck in Storming? Run a structured values discussion. Missing Norming? Create a team charter this week.
- ▪ Normalise conflict: Storming is development, not failure — suppressing it delays rather than prevents the issues
- ▪ Monitor for regression: Membership changes, scope shifts, or stress can send a Performing team back to Storming
- ▪ Build psychological safety: Teams can't reach peak performance unless members feel safe to speak up, fail, and challenge ideas
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 5

## Slide 13: Leadership Strategies by Stage

- Leadership Strategies by Stage
- Match your leadership style to what the team actually needs right now.
- ▪ Forming — Directive: Provide clear structure and guidance; define goals, roles, and expectations explicitly
- ▪ Storming — Coaching: Facilitate conflict resolution; acknowledge tension, separate people from problems
- ▪ Norming — Collaborative: Help establish shared processes and norms; encourage team ownership of decisions
- ▪ Performing — Delegative: Empower the team; step back from task direction and focus on removing obstacles
- ▪ Adjourning — Reflective: Celebrate achievements; run genuine retrospectives; capture lessons for future teams
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 5

## Slide 14: Tuckman in Practice — Challenges & Critical Elements

- Tuckman in Practice — Challenges & Critical Elements
- Understanding why teams stall — and what to address to move forward.
- The Three Critical Elements
- For teams to become productive, all three must be addressed:
- 📄 Content — What the team does — tasks and goals
- ⚙️ Process — How the team works — methods and procedures
- 💬 Feelings — How team members relate to each other
- ⚠️ Problem: Most teams focus only on Content, neglecting Process and Feelings — explaining why technically strong teams often underperform.
- Common Team Challenges
- ▸ Many teams get stuck in Storming — accept conflict as normal and never move forward
- ▸ Some never progress beyond Forming — lack of clear direction or leadership
- ▸ Incomplete Norming leads to regression — processes not fully established
- ▸ Teams only reach full effectiveness at Performing stage
- ▸ Timeline varies significantly — don't rush; address process AND feelings
- ▸ Monitor for regression during team changes or external stress

## Slide 26: Common Team Problems and Solutions

- Common Team Problems and Solutions
- Naming the specific behaviour makes it easier to address without feeling personal.
- Individual Behaviours
- ▪ Ego conflicts:Competing for credit rather than contributing to team success
- ▪ Self vs. group interest:Optimising individual grade at the expense of team quality
- ▪ Poor self-awareness:Not seeing how one's communication style lands with others
- ▪ These usually surface in Storming — address them directly rather than hoping they resolve themselves.
- Team Behaviours
- ▪ Defensive attitudes:Treating feedback as personal attack — fragile teams can't improve
- ▪ Getting off-task:Social conversation and tangents consume meeting time without advancing the work
- ▪ Unclear role boundaries:Two people assume someone else is handling something — nothing gets done
- ▪ Establishing ground rules in first meeting prevents most of these patterns from forming.
- FIT1055: IT Professional Practice and Ethics | Week 5

## Slide 27: Handling Difficult Team Behaviours

- Handling Difficult Team Behaviours
- Every team will face these — knowing how to respond is what makes the difference.
- Problem
- Solution
- Talkative member
- Use assertiveness skills — redirect with 'That's a great point, let's hear from others'
- Quiet / withdrawn member
- Apply good questioning techniques — use directed, open questions
- Argumentative member
- Use assertiveness and SBI feedback skills — focus on impact, not intent
- Free rider
- Set clear ground rules from the start; make individual accountability visible
- Group getting lost or digressing
- Use structured agendas and assertiveness to redirect: 'Let's park that and return to...'
- Indecisive group
- Use a facilitator; vote, use consensus, or delegate to team lead
- Feuding members
- Seek third-party mediation; separate content conflict from personal conflict
- 🚧 Common meeting traps: Hogging (talking too much) · Flogging (beating issues to death) · Frogging (topic jumping) · Bogging (getting stuck)
